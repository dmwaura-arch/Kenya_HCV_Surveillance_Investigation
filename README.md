# Kenya_HCV_Surveillance_Investigation
Investigating public evidence on HCV screening, active infection and transmission trends in Kenya.

## Investigating Evidence of New and Active Hepatitis C Virus Infections in Kenya

**Project focus:** Laboratory Medicine | Epidemiology | Public Health Surveillance | Data Analytics

## 1. Background

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) is a bloodborne virus that can cause acute and chronic liver disease. Understanding its burden requires distinguishing antibody positivity, current infection and newly acquired infection.

This project investigates publicly available evidence to assess whether new, active HCV infections are increasing in Kenya.

## 2. Research Question

**What do publicly available epidemiological and surveillance data reveal about trends in new and active HCV infections in Kenya?**

## 3. Objectives

- Examine reported HCV screening volumes and antibody positivity over time.
- Identify published evidence of HCV RNA-confirmed infections and incidence.
- Explore geographical and population-level patterns where comparable data are available.
- Assess data quality, reporting limitations and evidence gaps.
- Determine whether the evidence supports an increase, a decrease, no clear change, or an inconclusive trend.

## 4. Data Sources

This investigation uses public information from the following sources:

| Source | Intended use |
|---|---|
| [WHO Hepatitis Epidemiology Dashboard](https://data.who.int/dashboards/hepatitis/epidemiology?m49=404) | National indicators and annual estimates |
| [Kenya National Progress Report 2024](https://analytics.nsdcc.go.ke/estimates/PROGRESS-REPORT-2024.pdf) | Reported HCV screening indicators |
| [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) | Peer-reviewed Kenyan HCV research |
| [Kenya Ministry of Health](https://www.health.go.ke/) | Official public-health reports and surveillance information |

Figures will be recorded with their publication year, data period, population, indicator definition and source.

## 5. Methodology

The investigation follows a structured evidence-review and analysis process:

1. Identify and retrieve relevant public datasets, reports and research publications.
2. Extract annual HCV indicators and document their definitions.
3. Separate antibody screening results from HCV RNA-confirmed infections.
4. Distinguish observed data from modelled estimates.
5. Compare trends only where periods, populations and methods are sufficiently comparable.
6. Document limitations and assess the strength of the available evidence.
7. Develop evidence-based conclusions and recommendations.

## 6. Important Epidemiological Distinctions

- **Anti-HCV antibody positivity:** Indicates previous or current exposure to HCV.
- **HCV RNA positivity:** Indicates current viraemic infection.
- **New infection:** Requires evidence that infection was acquired during a defined period.
- **Incidence estimate:** Measures or estimates the occurrence of new infections over time.

These indicators are related but are not interchangeable.

## 7. Preliminary Findings

Preliminary analysis of reported HCV screening data extracted from Kenya's National Progress Report 2024 identified the following antibody screening positivity rates:

| Year | Tests conducted | Positive results | Screening positivity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020 | 63,353 | 1,674 | 2.64% |
| 2022 | 88,398 | 1,333 | 1.51% |
| 2023 | 103,465 | 1,792 | 1.73% |

Screening positivity declined between 2020 and 2023, although a modest increase occurred between 2022 and 2023.

Published Kenyan research has also documented HCV RNA-confirmed infections in selected study populations. However, these findings cannot automatically be generalised to the national population.

**Interpretation:** The available preliminary screening data do not establish whether new active HCV infections are increasing nationally. Screening positivity, the number of positive screening results, current viraemic infection and incidence represent different epidemiological measures.

**Data quality note:** The extracted figures and original indicator definitions require verification against the source report before the results can be considered final.
## 8. Limitations

- Publicly available data may not cover every county, population or year.
- Screening populations may change over time.
- Antibody positivity does not establish current infection.
- RNA-positive case counts alone do not necessarily measure incidence.
- Modelled estimates may differ from routinely observed laboratory data.
- Missing or inconsistent reporting may limit trend comparisons.
