# HCV Evidence Register — Kenya

## Purpose

This register documents publicly available evidence used to investigate trends in hepatitis C virus (HCV) screening, active infection and incidence in Kenya.

Every figure must be traceable to its original source. Unverified values must not be treated as established findings.

## Evidence Log

E001 — Kenya National Progress Report 2024

Period: 2020, 2022 and 2023

Finding: HCV screening positivity was reported as 2.6%, 1.5% and 1.7%, respectively.

Interpretation: These figures describe screening positivity, not national incidence of new active infections.

Source: https://analytics.nsdcc.go.ke/estimates/PROGRESS-REPORT-2024.pdf

E002 — Akiyama et al. (2019)

Period: Participants recruited from 2015 to 2017

Finding: 230 of 284 antibody-positive participants who underwent RNA testing had detectable HCV RNA.

Interpretation: This demonstrates active infection among participants in the studied population, not the national trend.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31540840/

E003 — Kenyan HCV Transmission-Network Study (2024)

Finding: The study investigated HCV transmission networks among people who inject drugs in Kenya.

Interpretation: Findings apply to the study population and should not be generalized to the whole country without supporting evidence.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39182826/

E004 — WHO Global Hepatitis Report 2026

Period: Report covers 2015–2024.

Status: Kenya-specific annual HCV incidence values still need to be extracted and verified.

Source: https://www.who.int/teams/global-hiv-hepatitis-and-stis-programmes/hepatitis/reports/global-hepatitis-report-2026

## Data Extraction Fields

For each source, record:

- Source title and organisation
- Direct URL or DOI
- Publication year
- Actual data collection period
- Indicator name and definition
- Reported value and unit
- Population and geographical coverage
- Whether data are observed, country-reported or modelled
- Uncertainty intervals, where available
- Important limitations
- Date accessed
- Verification status

## Interpretation Rules

1. Do not treat anti-HCV antibody positivity as proof of current infection.
2. Do not treat HCV RNA-positive case counts as incidence without appropriate supporting information.
3. Do not combine indicators with different definitions into a single trend.
4. Distinguish national estimates from findings in selected populations.
5. Document missing years and unavailable values rather than estimating them without a justified method.
6. Record source corrections and revisions when identified.

## Preliminary Research Question

Does the available public evidence support an increase in new, active HCV infections in Kenya?

## Current Status

Evidence collection is in progress. No conclusion about a recent nationwide increase in new active HCV infections has been established.

## Next Actions

- Retrieve and verify Kenya's national screening indicators.
- Obtain annual WHO HCV incidence estimates where available.
- Identify recent Kenyan studies with RNA-confirmed outcomes.
- Assess comparability across years, populations and data sources.
- Update the register before preparing the final analysis.
